A synopsis is a short plan of the research you intend to do. Your guide and your university's research committee use it to decide whether the topic is worth pursuing and whether you can complete it. A clear synopsis saves months later, because the thesis grows directly out of it.

Most Indian universities publish their own synopsis format, so always check yours first. The sections below are what almost every format asks for.

## 1. Title

Keep it specific and under about 20 words. It should name what you study, in whom or what, and where. "A study of working capital management in small textile units in Surat" tells the reader far more than "Working capital management".

## 2. Introduction and background

Two or three pages that explain the topic, why it matters now, and the context in India or your field. End with the gap: the thing that is not yet known.

## 3. Review of literature

Summarise the most relevant studies, grouped by theme rather than listed one by one. For each theme, say what has been found and what is still missing. This section shows the committee that you know the field.

## 4. Research gap and problem statement

One or two paragraphs that state the problem your research will address, in plain words. Everything that follows should connect back to it.

## 5. Objectives

Three to five objectives, each starting with a verb such as *to examine*, *to compare* or *to assess*. Each one should be something you can actually measure or answer.

## 6. Hypotheses or research questions

Quantitative studies usually state hypotheses that can be tested. Qualitative studies usually state research questions. Match them one to one with your objectives where you can.

## 7. Research methodology

Explain the research design, the population and sample, the sample size and how you chose it, the data you will collect and how, and the tools you will use to analyse it. This is the section committees question most, so be precise.

## 8. Scope and limitations

Say what the study covers and what it deliberately leaves out, such as a region, a time period or a type of organisation.

## 9. Chapter plan and timeline

List the chapters you expect your thesis to have and a realistic timeline for each stage.

## 10. References

Every source cited in the synopsis, in the citation style your university asks for. See our guide to [citation styles](/resources/citation-styles-at-a-glance).

## Common reasons a synopsis is sent back

- The objectives do not match the problem statement.
- The methodology is vague about the sample or the analysis.
- The literature review lists studies without showing the gap.
- The title promises more than the study can deliver.
- The references are incomplete or in mixed styles.
