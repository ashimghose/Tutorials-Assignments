Starting guitar is exciting, and the first month decides whether it becomes a habit. Here is what you need, how to set up for an online class, and a simple practice plan for the first four weeks.

![What you need for online guitar classes: an acoustic guitar, a clip-on tuner, picks and a phone or laptop on a stand](/images/illustrations/guitar-starter-kit.svg)

## What you need

- **An acoustic guitar.** A basic one is fine to start. Children under about 10 are often more comfortable with a smaller (3/4 size) guitar.
- **A clip-on tuner, or a free tuner app.** A guitar slips out of tune easily, and playing out of tune makes everything sound wrong.
- **A few picks (plectrums).** Thin or medium picks are easiest for strumming.
- **A phone, tablet or laptop on a stand,** placed so the teacher can see both your hands, and earphones so you can hear clearly.

## Setting up for an online class

- Sit on a chair without arms, in a quiet room with light in front of you, not behind.
- Tune the guitar before the class starts, so no class time is lost.
- Turn off noise cancellation in the video call app if you can find the setting; it can cut out the sound of the guitar.
- Keep a notebook nearby for chords and strumming patterns.

## Week 1: holding, tuning and your first chord

- Learn the names of the six strings: E, A, D, G, B, e (thickest to thinnest).
- Practise **Em**: two fingers, and all six strings ring.
- Strum slowly down, four beats at a time, and keep a steady count.

## Week 2: C and G

- Add **C** and **G**. Press just behind the metal fret, with your fingertips, not the pads.
- Change slowly between Em and C, then C and G. Speed comes later; clean changes come first.

## Week 3: D and your first strumming pattern

- Add **D**. Now you know the four chords that play hundreds of songs.
- Learn a simple pattern: down, down-up, up-down-up. Count it out loud.

## Week 4: your first song

- Put the chords together in a song you know. Your teacher will pick one that uses Em, C, G and D.
- Record yourself on your phone once a week. Listening back shows progress you might not notice.

## How much to practise

**Fifteen to twenty minutes a day** beats one long session a week. Sore fingertips in the first two weeks are normal; they toughen up quickly. If something hurts beyond the fingertips, stop and ask your teacher about your posture.
